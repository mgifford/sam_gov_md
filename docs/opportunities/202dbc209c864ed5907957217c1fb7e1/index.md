"Sandblast one side of shim while holding it flat against a steel plate with air pressure set between 80 to 90 psi, or equivalent process. Use clean, dry aluminum oxide grit No. 24 (Norton MCA-1360, 24 grit or equal), or equivalent process, to obtain a finish of 100 to 200 RHR. This will cause the shim to distort and take a convex shape. Turn shim over and sandblast the other side using the same procedure, until the shim again takes a flat shape. Next, sandblast one side of the shim, using clean, dry aluminum oxide grit No. 80, using the same procedure, or equivalent process. Check the shim for flatness. If this last step has caused any distortion (convexity), again turn the shim over and lightly re-blast the other side with No. 24 grit until shim is flat. NOTE: Minor distortion (convexity or twisting) is acceptable, if shim can be flattened with light hand pressure. Now, hand work the side of the shim that was just sandblasted with No. 80 grit, with Scotch-Brite Heavy Duty Hand Pad, No. 7440, or equal, to restore a surface finish of 63 RHR or less. Use no oil or any other material that might contaminate the shim and affect the bonding surface on the other side. Minor random surface pitting is acceptable, as long as the desired surface finish can be obtained. This procedure will produce a flat shim with a surface finish of 100 to 200 RHR on one side (for epoxy bonding) and a surface finish of 63 RHR or less on the other (for ball seat sealing).";
