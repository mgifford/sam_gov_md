Downtime is defined as any time the equipment is not available for patient testing due to malfunction, unacceptable quality control, software system errors, or hardware failures. This includes time used for end-user troubleshooting, conversations with technical support or on-site engineer repair/support. Standard acceptable performance for the life of the contract, following acceptance as defined in section below, is defined as a period of 30 consecutive calendar days by operating in conformation with the contractor's technical specification without prolonged downtime (>4 hours) per instance more than 3 times within 30 days or more than 10 instances of downtime (of any length of time) within 30 days. In the event the equipment or an assay does not meet the standard acceptable performance for 30 consecutive days, the government will notify the contractor of the ongoing unacceptable performance and the performance will be monitored for an addition 90 consecutive days. If after 120 calendar days from the initial date of unacceptability, the vendor does not resolve the performance concerns to meet the governments expectations, the government, through the contracting officer, may request a replacement of the equipment / assay or terminate the order upon mutual agreement. Removal of the existing instrument by the Contractor shall be performed within 30 days following the acceptance of replacement equipment without additional charge to the government. Enhancements to the contractor's equipment shall be delivered to the government site and installed by the contractor within 60 days of their issuance or date of first commercial availability. SYSTEM DATA MANAGEMENT HARDWARE / SOFTWARE The Data Management System must include a test environment and product account. The contractor is responsible for assisting the government with the initial virtual server build and on-going maintenance of both servers. The system must have sufficient data storage for 2 years of workload data and provide immediate access to more than 7,500 patient records per day, including instrument identification (houses PHI and PII). The Contractor s equipment shall be compatible, or have the ability to, act as a pass-through to the government s Data Innovations (DI) Instrument Manager for instances when the government s DI is the primary rule management software. DI connection is not required for vendor s who connect directly to the government s LIS and when the vendor s middleware is the primary rule management software. As a responsible owner of the track operating system, the contractor is responsible for assisting the government with track software modifications (i.e. routing rules) when 3rd-party connections are added and/or modified. The vendor must provide all printers, including all components for printer functionality (printer drivers) and printing (excluding toner and printer paper). The contractor and lab information manager must have remote access connectivity for troubleshooting systems, interface connections, and analyzers. Contractors require network access to the instruments and to the interface manager. The contractor s system software must include a comprehensive Quality Control Data Manager or provide compatibility with Bio-Rad Unity Real Time Quality Control (QC) program. The proposed system must include real-time reports, printable monthly reports, and peer-group evaluations with like controls, instruments and assays. All software and software licenses, interfaces, and hardware (e.g. servers, printers, print drivers, monitors, UPS, etc.) shall be included with cost of hardware required disclosed for removal if needed. Procurement of systems with unsupported operating systems is prohibited.
