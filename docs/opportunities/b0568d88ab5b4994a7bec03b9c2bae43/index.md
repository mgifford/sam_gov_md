For this current project, the selected A/E will have 180 calendar days to identify, classify, and design remedies for critical structural deficiencies. This effort will be accomplished through both field investigations and review of prior evaluations. Building structural deficiencies are typically classified into Major and Minor levels based on their impact on safety, load-bearing capacity, and habitability. These deficiencies represent flaws in design, construction, current condition or materials that can lead to performance issues, including catastrophic failure, if not addressed. Major Structural Deficiencies: These are significant issues in key load-bearing components: Foundation Deficiencies: Include settling, sinking, or heaving of the foundation. Signs include large stair-step cracks in masonry, diagonal cracks, and doors/windows that stick or won't close. Framing/Load-Bearing Defects: Involve undersized framing members, missing hardware, or poor-quality work during construction. Material Degradation: The weakening of materials, such as rotting wood, rusting steel, or spalling concrete, often caused by water infiltration. Water Intrusion: A major, persistent cause of structural decline, leading to oxidation in steel, rot in wooden components, and weakening of concrete foundations. Overloading: Damage caused by loads exceeding the building's original design capacity. Minor Structural Deficiencies: These are small, localized flaws that do not immediately compromise the safety or structural integrity of the entire building. Examples include small cracks in plaster, minor surface corrosion, or uneven finishes. These should still be addressed to prevent them from becoming major issues over time. Common items include: Cracks: Diagonal cracks (wider than 1/4 inch) in foundations or walls, and horizontal cracks in masonry. Movement/Settlement: Sloping and uneven floors and walking surfaces, sagging roofs, and bulging/leaning walls. Gaps: Visible gaps where walls meet ceilings or floors, indicating significant shifting. Failed Components: Deteriorating brickwork, failed gutters causing water damage, and oxidizing steel surfaces. Inadequately secured overhead appurtenances, such as pipes and ducts. Inadequately secured free-standing appurtenances, such as racks and electrical gear. Common FboNotice causes of Structural Deficiencies typically arise from: Original Design Deficiencies: Incorrect structural plans, such as missing or undersized components. Original Construction Deficiency: Poor quality work, improper installation, and not building to code. Material Deficiencies: Using inferior materials, such as defective concrete or weak timber. Operational/Maintenance Deficiencies: Neglecting repairs, allowing water to damage components, or poor drainage around the foundation. This project will evaluate and plan appropriate repairs and/or modifications to all hospital structures, equipment, and appurtenances that need structural repairs and/or fortification.
